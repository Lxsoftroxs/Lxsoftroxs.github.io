<p class="project-callout">This is a personal project, not official guidance. In a real emergency, follow Santa Cruz County OES, local alerts and <a href="https://www.ready.gov/">ready.gov</a>.</p>

## Why

Most preparedness material is written for nowhere in particular. This guide is about one place: Santa Cruz, with its mountains, single highways and coastline. Two other projects grew out of it. The [Meshtastic net]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}) is the communications layer, and the cold water station started as a water-filtration idea.

## What the guide covers

**1. Trusted information.** Which sources to believe during a crisis, and how to handle the panic and disinformation that come with one.

**2. Communications.** A NOAA Weather Radio receiver (KEC-49 covers the area) and the local amateur radio repeaters (K6BJ, W6SLG). A cheap scanner or a licensed handheld turns those into a lifeline when phones are down.

**3. Shelter.** Different emergencies need different shelter. For fallout, what matters is mass and distance between you and the outside: below ground or the center of a large concrete building, away from windows, and staying put rather than fleeing into the open. Check dose rates before you leave.

**4. Water and supplies.** Stored water first. A carbon filter fixes taste and chlorine but doesn't remove radioactive contamination. Reverse osmosis removes many of those ions, which is why water prep is still an open question in this guide. Fog drip and open water catchments count as contaminated after fallout.

**5. Medical basics.** A stocked kit, enough of any prescription medications, and the basics of radiation medicine: what potassium iodide does and doesn't do, and when to take it.

**6. Leaving.** After the first one to four weeks, if you need to move: Hwy 1 south toward Monterey is usually the best route out. Avoid Hwy 17 and Hwy 9 if the Bay Area has been hit, since everyone else will be on them.

**7. Community.** How people have organized themselves when institutions failed, using examples like Sarajevo, post-Katrina New Orleans and Puerto Rico after María. Food banks, community farms and existing homeless-services networks are distribution systems that already exist.
